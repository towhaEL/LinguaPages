Chapter Four: The Silent Harbour

I still remember the day that my father took me to the Cemetery of Forgotten Books for the first time. The early summer fog lingered in the cobblestones of Rambla de Santa Mónica like wet spun glass, veiling the gas lanterns in spectral halos.

We stepped into the vaulted darkness, and my senses were enveloped by the intoxicating scent of ancient glue, damp parchment, and resting memories. Countless shelves ascended toward the skylights like cathedrals of untended wisdom, housing millions of discarded souls that waited for a living hand to bring their whispered lines back to wakefulness.

"Every book you see here has a soul," my father spoke softly, resting a worn palm against my shoulder. "The soul of the person who wrote it and the souls of those who read it and dreamed with it."
