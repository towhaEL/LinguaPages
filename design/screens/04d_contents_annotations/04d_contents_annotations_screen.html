CHAPTER IV p. 142

The Silent Harbour

The early summer fog lingered in the cobbles of Rambla de Santa Mónica like wet spun glass. I walked past the shuttered bookshops with the leather satchel clutched under my arm, still warm from the morning bread my father had handed me at dawn.

In Barcelona, secrets have their own geography. They do not reside in church vaults or government safes; they slip between the loose bricks of courtyards, nestling inside the mold of seventeenth-century library shelves...

"Every book you see here has been someone’s dearest secret, someone’s sleepless night," Isaac had murmured to me, leading the way into the labyrinth.

The Shadow of the Wind
